## Where to get tested for free or cheap

| Option | Typical cost | Good to know |
|---|---|---|
| Health department STD clinic | Free to low cost | Often walk-in; very private |
| Planned Parenthood | Sliding scale; takes most insurance | Many offer same-day appointments |
| Community health center | Sliding scale by income | Find one at findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov |
| Title X family planning clinic | Free to low cost | Confidential care, including for teens |
| College health center | Often free for students | Ask about anonymous options |
| LGBTQ+ community center | Often free | Many offer HIV and syphilis rapid tests |
| Free mail-in programs | Free | Availability depends on your state |

## Find testing by ZIP code

The CDC's [GetTested](https://gettested.cdc.gov/) directory lists clinics that test for HIV, STDs and hepatitis. Listings show fees, so you can pick a free or low-cost site.

## If you have insurance

Under the Affordable Care Act, most health plans must cover screening the USPSTF recommends, with no copay, when you use an in-network provider. That includes:

- HIV screening for everyone 15 to 65, and others at higher risk
- Chlamydia and gonorrhea screening for sexually active women 24 and under, and older women at higher risk
- Syphilis screening for people at higher risk and during pregnancy
- Hepatitis B screening for people at higher risk, and hepatitis C screening for all adults 18 to 79

Medicaid covers STD testing in every state. Testing because of symptoms may involve a copay.

## Free home tests

Some state and national programs mail free HIV self-tests, and in some states free STD collection kits. Search your state health department's website, or check GetTested for mail-in options.

Paid lab-processed home kits are another option if privacy or convenience matters more than cost. Compare them on our [at-home test kits](/std-testing/best-at-home-std-tests/) page.

## Tips for your visit

- Ask which infections they test for, and which body sites. Oral and anal sex need throat and rectal swabs.
- Ask how you'll get results and how long they take.
- Mention any exposure date so they can tell you if you need a repeat test.
