## Common result terms

| On your report | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Negative, not detected | No infection found | Retest if you were still in the window period |
| Positive, detected | Infection found | Get treatment and tell recent partners |
| Reactive | A screening test reacted | Get the confirmatory test |
| Non-reactive | Screening test didn't react | Same as negative |
| Indeterminate, equivocal | Result unclear | Repeat the test, usually in 2 to 4 weeks |
| Invalid, QNS | Sample couldn't be tested (QNS = quantity not sufficient) | Collect a new sample |

For privacy and convenience, an [at-home test kit](/go/k1/) processed by a certified lab is another option.

## Chlamydia, gonorrhea and trichomoniasis

These use NAATs, which give a simple **detected** or **not detected** result for each sample.

- **Detected:** the infection is there. All three are curable with treatment from a clinician.
- **Not detected:** no infection in that sample. A urine test doesn't check the throat or rectum.

## HIV

- **Non-reactive:** negative. If you tested inside the [window period](/std-testing/window-period/), test again once it has passed.
- **Reactive:** a *preliminary* positive. A lab must confirm it with a different test before it's a diagnosis.
- **Confirmed positive:** start care with a clinician quickly. Modern HIV care lets people with HIV live long, healthy lives and, once the virus is undetectable, not pass it on through sex.

## Syphilis

Syphilis results usually come as **two kinds of blood test**:

- **Treponemal test** (such as TP-PA or EIA): shows if you've *ever* had syphilis. It usually stays positive for life, even after a cure.
- **Nontreponemal test** (RPR or VDRL): shows *activity*. It's reported with a **titer**, such as 1:8. A higher second number means more activity.

Both positive usually means current or recent infection needing treatment. Treponemal positive with nontreponemal negative can mean past, treated syphilis, or very early infection; a clinician decides which.

After treatment, the titer should fall at least fourfold (for example, from 1:32 to 1:8) over the following months.

## Herpes (HSV-1 and HSV-2)

- **Swab of a sore (PCR):** the most reliable result. Positive confirms herpes and its type.
- **IgG blood test:** reported as an index value. The CDC notes that **HSV-2 index values from 1.1 to 3.0 are often false positives** and should be confirmed with a second test.
- **IgM tests** are not useful and shouldn't be used to diagnose herpes.

## Hepatitis C

- **Antibody negative:** no infection, unless the exposure was very recent.
- **Antibody positive:** you've been infected at some point. An **HCV RNA** test shows whether the virus is still there.
- **RNA detected:** current infection. Hepatitis C is curable; see a clinician about treatment.
- **RNA not detected:** the infection has cleared or been cured.

## Hepatitis B

| Marker | Positive means |
|---|---|
| HBsAg (surface antigen) | Current infection |
| Anti-HBs (surface antibody) | Protected, from vaccination or past recovery |
| Anti-HBc (core antibody) | Infected at some point, now or in the past |

## After a positive result

1. **See a clinician for treatment.** Most bacterial STDs are curable. Don't self-treat.
2. **Tell partners** so they can test and get treated. Health departments can tell partners for you without using your name.
3. **Avoid sex** until you and your partners finish treatment.
4. **Retest** about 3 months after treatment for chlamydia, gonorrhea and trichomoniasis.
