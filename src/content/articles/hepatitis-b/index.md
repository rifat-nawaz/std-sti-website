## How hepatitis B spreads

The hepatitis B virus (HBV) spreads through **blood, semen and vaginal fluids**:

- Sex without a condom with someone who has hepatitis B
- Sharing needles, syringes or other drug equipment
- From a parent with hepatitis B to their baby at birth
- Sharing razors or toothbrushes, or needle-stick injuries

It doesn't spread through kissing, hugging, coughing, sharing food or breastfeeding.

## Acute and chronic infection

| | Acute | Chronic |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The first 6 months after infection | Infection lasting more than 6 months |
| Who | Most adults clear the virus in this time | About 9 in 10 infected babies, and about 5 in 100 infected adults |
| Symptoms | Often none; sometimes tiredness, nausea, belly pain, dark urine, yellow skin or eyes | Usually none for years |
| Risk | Rarely, sudden liver failure | Liver scarring (cirrhosis) and liver cancer |

## Testing

Hepatitis B is tested with a blood test. The CDC recommends a **triple panel** for adults:

- **HBsAg:** shows current infection
- **Anti-HBs:** shows protection, from the vaccine or past recovery
- **Anti-HBc:** shows past or current infection

The CDC recommends this test **at least once for every adult**, and in every pregnancy. See [how to read hepatitis B results](/std-testing/reading-results/).

## The vaccine

- Recommended for **all adults 19 to 59**, and adults 60 and older with risk factors or who want it.
- Given as **2 or 3 shots**, depending on the brand.
- All babies get it starting at birth.

If you haven't been vaccinated, ask for it at your next STD test.

## Treatment

- **Acute hepatitis B:** usually rest, fluids and monitoring.
- **Chronic hepatitis B:** regular liver checks, and for many people, daily antiviral pills such as tenofovir or entecavir.

People with chronic hepatitis B should avoid alcohol, check with a clinician before taking new medicines or supplements, and get vaccinated against hepatitis A.

## Protecting partners

- Partners and people you live with should be tested and vaccinated.
- Use condoms until partners are protected.
- Cover cuts and don't share razors or toothbrushes.
