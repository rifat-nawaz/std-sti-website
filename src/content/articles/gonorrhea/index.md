## What is gonorrhea?

Gonorrhea is an infection caused by the bacterium *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*. It spreads through vaginal, anal and oral sex, and from a pregnant person to their baby at birth.

It can infect the cervix, urethra, rectum, throat and eyes. You can have it in more than one place at once.

## Symptoms

Many people have **no symptoms**, especially women and anyone with a throat or rectal infection. When symptoms happen, they usually start within 2 weeks.

| Where | Possible symptoms |
|---|---|
| Penis | Burning when peeing; white, yellow or green discharge; painful or swollen testicles |
| Vagina or cervix | Burning when peeing, more discharge, bleeding between periods |
| Rectum | Discharge, itching, soreness, bleeding, painful bowel movements |
| Throat | Usually none; sometimes a sore throat |

## Complications

Without treatment, gonorrhea can:

- Spread to the uterus and fallopian tubes (pelvic inflammatory disease), causing pelvic pain, ectopic pregnancy and infertility
- Cause painful swelling of the tube near the testicles (epididymitis), which rarely affects fertility
- Spread through the blood to the joints and skin (disseminated gonococcal infection), a medical emergency
- Raise the chance of getting or passing on HIV

## Testing

Gonorrhea is tested with a **NAAT**, the same test used for chlamydia. Most labs test for both from one sample.

- **Urine** or a **vaginal swab** for genital infection
- **Throat swab** if you've had oral sex
- **Rectal swab** if you've had receptive anal sex

Test about **2 weeks after a possible exposure**. See the [window period calculator](/std-testing/window-period/).

**Who should test:** sexually active women 24 and under every year, older women at higher risk, and gay and bisexual men at least once a year at every site used for sex (every 3 to 6 months if at higher risk).

## Treatment

Under current CDC guidelines, gonorrhea is treated with:

- **Ceftriaxone 500 mg as a single injection** (1 g for people weighing 150 kg or more)
- Plus **doxycycline for 7 days** if chlamydia hasn't been ruled out

After treatment:

1. **Avoid sex for 7 days** after treatment, and until partners are treated.
2. **Tell partners from the last 60 days** so they can be tested and treated.
3. **Throat infection?** Get a test of cure 7 to 14 days after treatment.
4. **Retest about 3 months later**, even if partners were treated.

If symptoms continue after treatment, go back to your clinician. They may test for antibiotic resistance.

## Antibiotic resistance

Gonorrhea has become resistant to almost every antibiotic used against it. Ceftriaxone is the last reliable option recommended in the US. Health agencies track resistance closely, and completing follow-up testing helps catch treatment failures early.

## Prevention

- Use condoms for vaginal and anal sex, and condoms or dental dams for oral sex.
- Test regularly and before new relationships.
- If you're a gay or bisexual man or a transgender woman who had a bacterial STD in the past year, ask about **doxy-PEP**. It works less well for gonorrhea than for chlamydia and syphilis, but still lowers risk.
