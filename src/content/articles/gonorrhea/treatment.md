## Where to go

| Option | Good to know |
|---|---|
| Health department STD clinic | Often free; usually treats the same day |
| Urgent care | Open evenings and weekends |
| Your doctor | Can also arrange follow-up tests |
| Planned Parenthood or community clinic | Sliding-scale fees |

See [free and low-cost testing](/std-testing/free-testing/).

## What happens at the visit

1. **Questions** about symptoms, partners, the kind of sex you've had and possible pregnancy.
2. **Samples** from each body site that may be infected: urine or genital swab, throat and rectum.
3. **Treatment**, chosen by the clinician based on current guidelines. It's often given before you leave.
4. **Other tests**, including chlamydia, HIV and syphilis.

## Why you shouldn't self-treat

Gonorrhea has become resistant to almost every antibiotic used against it. Leftover pills, online antibiotics or medicine meant for something else are likely to fail, and a failed treatment helps resistance spread.

## After treatment

- **Avoid sex for 7 days** after treatment, and until partners are treated and symptoms are gone.
- **Tell partners from the last 60 days.** Ask about expedited partner therapy.
- **Throat infection?** Return for a test of cure 7 to 14 days later.
- **Retest about 3 months later**, even if partners were treated.

Read more about [retesting after gonorrhea](/gonorrhea/test-of-cure/).
