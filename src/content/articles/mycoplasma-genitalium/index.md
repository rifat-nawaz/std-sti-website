## What is Mycoplasma genitalium?

*Mycoplasma genitalium* is a very small bacterium that spreads through sex. It was identified in the 1980s, but tests only became widely available in the US in 2019.

## Symptoms

Many people have none. When symptoms happen:

| People with a penis | People with a vagina |
|---|---|
| Burning when peeing | Discharge |
| Watery discharge | Bleeding after sex or between periods |
| Pain in the urethra | Pelvic pain, pain during sex |

Mgen is a leading cause of **urethritis that doesn't go away** after standard treatment. It's also linked to cervicitis and pelvic inflammatory disease.

## Who should be tested

The CDC recommends testing with a NAAT for:

- Men with urethritis that continues or comes back after treatment
- Women with cervicitis that continues or comes back
- Consideration in women with pelvic inflammatory disease

Testing people without symptoms isn't recommended.

## Getting treated

Mgen is curable, but it's often resistant to commonly used antibiotics. Some labs can test which medicines will work. A clinician should always choose the treatment.

After a diagnosis:

1. **Don't self-treat.** Leftover or online antibiotics can be the wrong medicine, and a failed treatment lets the infection keep spreading.
2. **Follow your clinician's instructions** and finish everything they prescribe, even if you feel better.
3. **Avoid sex** until you and your partners have finished treatment and your clinician says it's safe.
4. **Tell recent partners** so they can be tested and treated. See [how to tell a partner](/guides/telling-a-partner/).
