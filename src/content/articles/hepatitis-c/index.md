## How hepatitis C spreads

Hepatitis C virus (HCV) spreads through contact with **infected blood**:

- Sharing needles, syringes or other drug equipment (the most common route in the US)
- From parent to baby during birth
- Unsterile tattoo or piercing equipment
- Sharing razors, nail clippers or toothbrushes
- **Sex**, less commonly. The risk is higher with anal sex, rough sex, sex during a period, multiple partners, other STDs, and for people with HIV

## Symptoms

Most people have **no symptoms** for years. Some notice tiredness, nausea or yellow skin or eyes within a few months of infection.

Over 20 to 30 years, untreated chronic hepatitis C can cause liver scarring, liver failure and liver cancer.

## Testing

Testing happens in two steps:

1. **Antibody test:** shows whether you've ever been infected. It can take 8 to 11 weeks after exposure to turn positive.
2. **HCV RNA test:** if the antibody test is positive, this shows whether the virus is still there. It can detect infection 1 to 2 weeks after exposure.

**Who should test:**

- Every adult at least once
- Every pregnancy
- People who inject drugs, regularly
- People with HIV, and gay and bisexual men with HIV, at least once a year

For privacy and convenience, an [at-home test kit](/go/k1/) processed by a certified lab is another option.

## Getting treated

Hepatitis C is **curable** in more than 95% of people. Treatment is prescribed and monitored by a clinician, and primary care clinicians can now treat it, not only liver specialists. Medicaid and most insurance plans cover it.

After treatment, a blood test confirms the cure. You can be infected again, so keep avoiding shared needles and get tested after any new exposure.

## Prevention

- Never share needles or drug equipment. Syringe services programs offer free supplies.
- Use condoms, especially for anal sex.
- Don't share razors or toothbrushes.
- Choose licensed tattoo and piercing studios.
