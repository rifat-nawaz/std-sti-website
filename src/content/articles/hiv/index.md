## What is HIV?

HIV (human immunodeficiency virus) attacks CD4 cells, which help the body fight infections. Without treatment, it slowly weakens the immune system.

HIV spreads through blood, semen, pre-cum, vaginal fluids, rectal fluids and breast milk. Most infections in the US come from anal or vaginal sex without a condom or PrEP, or from sharing needles.

## Symptoms

| Stage | What can happen |
|---|---|
| Acute HIV (2 to 4 weeks after infection) | Flu-like illness: fever, rash, sore throat, swollen glands, muscle aches. Some people have no symptoms |
| Chronic HIV | Often no symptoms for years while the virus slowly damages the immune system |
| AIDS | Severe infections and some cancers, weight loss, ongoing fever |

Symptoms can't confirm or rule out HIV. Only a test can.

## Testing

| Test | Sample | Conclusive after |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleic acid test (NAT) | Lab blood draw | 33 days |
| Antigen/antibody test | Lab blood draw | 45 days |
| Antigen/antibody rapid test | Finger prick | 90 days |
| Antibody test, including oral self-tests | Finger prick or mouth swab | 90 days |

Source: CDC. A **reactive** result on any screening test needs a confirmatory lab test. See [how to read results](/std-testing/reading-results/).

**Who should test:** everyone aged 15 to 65 at least once, everyone who is pregnant, and anyone at higher risk at least once a year. Gay and bisexual men may benefit from testing every 3 to 6 months.

## Living with HIV

HIV can't be cured, but it can be controlled. A clinician prescribes and monitors HIV care.

- Care should start as soon as possible after diagnosis, often the same day.
- Most people in care reach an **undetectable viral load** within about 6 months.
- With ongoing care, people with HIV can expect a near-normal lifespan.

## Undetectable = Untransmittable (U=U)

People with HIV who stay in care and keep an undetectable viral load **do not pass HIV to partners through sex**. This is backed by large studies and endorsed by the CDC.

## Prevention

- **PrEP:** prescription medicine that prevents HIV before exposure. It reduces the risk from sex by about 99% when used as prescribed. Ask a clinician if it fits you.
- **PEP:** emergency medicine started within 72 hours after a possible exposure. [How to get PEP](/hiv/pep/).
- **Condoms** every time.
- **Testing and care** for partners: U=U means staying in care is prevention.
- **Never share** needles, syringes or other drug equipment.
