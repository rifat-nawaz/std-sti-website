## What PEP is

PEP stands for **post-exposure prophylaxis**. It's a short course of HIV medicines that stops the virus from taking hold in your body after a possible exposure.

It's for emergencies. It is not meant for regular use.

## When you might need PEP

Consider PEP if, in the last 72 hours, you:

- Had vaginal or anal sex without a condom, or a condom broke, with a partner who has HIV or whose status you don't know
- Shared needles or other drug equipment
- Were sexually assaulted

PEP may not be needed if your partner has HIV and has had an undetectable viral load for at least 6 months. A clinician can help you decide. When in doubt, go and ask.

## How to get PEP today

1. **Go now.** An emergency room or urgent care center can start PEP day or night. Sexual health clinics and some pharmacies also provide it.
2. **Ask for it by name.** Say: "I had a possible HIV exposure and I'd like to start PEP."
3. **Expect a quick HIV test** and some blood tests. You should be able to start PEP without waiting for most results.
4. **Leave with a plan** for the full 28 days of medicine and follow-up visits.

## What taking PEP involves

- **A 4-week course** of prescription HIV medicine. Your clinician explains how to take it.
- **Take it exactly as prescribed** and finish the course. Missed medicine lowers protection.
- **Follow-up HIV tests** in the weeks and months after, as your clinician advises.
- **Use condoms** until your follow-up tests confirm you're HIV-negative.

## Cost and help paying

Most insurance plans, including Medicaid, cover PEP. If you're uninsured or the copay is high, ask about drug-maker assistance programs. After sexual assault, state victim compensation programs can cover costs.

## After PEP: think about PrEP

If you might be exposed again, ask about **PrEP**: prescription medicine that prevents HIV before exposure. Many people move straight from PEP to PrEP without a gap.

## How PEP fits with STD testing

An HIV exposure is also a possible exposure to other STDs. Ask to be tested for chlamydia, gonorrhea and syphilis at the PEP visit or soon after. Use our [window period calculator](/std-testing/window-period/) to plan when each test will be reliable.
