## Your first steps

1. **Confirm the diagnosis.** A reactive screening test needs a lab test to confirm it.
2. **Find an HIV care provider.** Your testing site can usually refer you the same day.
3. **Ask about help paying.** Insurance, Medicaid and the Ryan White program can cover care.

## What happens at the first visits

- **Viral load:** how much virus is in your blood
- **CD4 count:** how strong your immune system is
- **Resistance test:** helps the clinician choose the right care
- **Other checks:** hepatitis B and C, other STDs, kidney and liver function, vaccines

Your clinician then starts your HIV care plan and explains how to follow it.

## Staying on track

- **Follow your care plan every day.** Missed care lets the virus rebound.
- **Go to follow-up visits** to check your viral load. Early on these are frequent; later they're usually a few times a year.
- **Tell your clinician** about any other medicines or supplements you take.

## Undetectable = Untransmittable

When care keeps your viral load undetectable for at least 6 months, you **do not pass HIV to partners through sex**. Keep up with care to stay undetectable.

## Telling partners

Recent partners should be tested and may benefit from PEP or PrEP. You can tell them yourself or ask the health department to notify them anonymously. See [telling a partner](/guides/telling-a-partner/).

## Looking after yourself

A diagnosis can feel overwhelming. Many clinics have counselors and peer navigators who have been through it. If you feel hopeless, call or text 988.
