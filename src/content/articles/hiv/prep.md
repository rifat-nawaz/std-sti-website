## How PrEP works

PrEP medicine stops HIV from taking hold if you're exposed. It comes in more than one form, including pills and injections given every few months. Your clinician will help you choose the option that fits your life.

## How well it works

- **Sex:** reduces the risk of getting HIV by about 99% when used as prescribed.
- **Injection drug use:** reduces the risk by at least 74%.

PrEP works best when used exactly as prescribed. Missing doses or appointments lowers protection.

## How to ask for PrEP

Say: "I'd like to talk about PrEP." You can ask:

- Your primary care clinician
- A sexual health clinic or health department
- A telehealth PrEP service
- Some pharmacies

## What to expect

1. **An HIV test** before you start, because PrEP isn't for people who already have HIV.
2. **Other lab tests**, such as kidney function and hepatitis B, depending on the option.
3. **Regular follow-up**, usually every few months, with HIV and STD tests.

## Cost

The USPSTF gives PrEP an "A" grade, so most insurance plans must cover it and the related tests with no cost sharing. Assistance programs help people without insurance.
