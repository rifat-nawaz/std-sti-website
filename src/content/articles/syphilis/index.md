## What is syphilis?

Syphilis is an infection caused by the bacterium *Treponema pallidum*. It spreads through direct contact with a syphilis sore or rash during vaginal, anal or oral sex. It can also pass from a pregnant person to their baby.

## The stages of syphilis

| Stage | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | About 3 weeks after exposure (10 to 90 days) | A firm, round, usually painless sore where the infection entered. Heals in 3 to 6 weeks without treatment |
| Secondary | Weeks to months later | A rash, often on the palms and soles; fever, swollen glands, sore throat, patchy hair loss, tiredness |
| Latent | After symptoms fade | No symptoms, but the infection remains. Can last for years |
| Tertiary | 10 to 30 years later, in some untreated people | Damage to the heart, brain, nerves and other organs |

**Syphilis can affect the brain (neurosyphilis), eyes (ocular syphilis) or ears (otosyphilis) at any stage.** These need urgent treatment to prevent permanent damage.

## Testing

Syphilis is diagnosed with **blood tests**. Two types are used together:

- A **treponemal test** shows whether you've ever been infected.
- A **nontreponemal test** (RPR or VDRL) shows current activity, reported as a titer such as 1:16.

A clinician can also test fluid from a sore directly.

Blood tests can take **up to 12 weeks** after exposure to turn positive. If you test earlier and it's negative, test again. See [how to read syphilis results](/std-testing/reading-results/).

**Who should test:** everyone who is pregnant, gay and bisexual men at least once a year, people with HIV, and anyone with a new sore or rash or a partner with syphilis.

## Getting treated

Syphilis is **curable** at every stage. A clinician chooses the treatment based on the stage and on whether you're pregnant, and checks that it worked.

After a diagnosis:

1. **Don't self-treat.** Leftover or online antibiotics can be the wrong medicine, and a failed treatment lets the infection keep spreading.
2. **Follow your clinician's instructions** and finish everything they prescribe, even if you feel better.
3. **Avoid sex** until you and your partners have finished treatment and your clinician says it's safe.
4. **Tell recent partners** so they can be tested and treated. See [how to tell a partner](/guides/telling-a-partner/).
5. **Go to follow-up blood tests.** Your clinician will repeat the RPR or VDRL test over the following months to make sure the titer falls.

Some people feel feverish or achy for a day after starting treatment. Tell your clinician if this happens.

## Syphilis in pregnancy

Syphilis can pass to the baby and cause miscarriage, stillbirth or lifelong health problems. Cases of congenital syphilis have risen sharply in the US. Get tested at the first prenatal visit, and again in the third trimester and at delivery if your provider advises.

## Prevention

- Use condoms, though they only protect the area they cover.
- Test regularly if you have new or multiple partners.
- If you're a gay or bisexual man or a transgender woman who had a bacterial STD in the last year, ask a clinician what prevention options fit you, including options taken after sex.
