## Step 1: Staging

Your clinician uses your symptoms, history and blood tests to work out the stage: primary, secondary, early latent, late latent, or syphilis affecting the eyes, ears or nervous system. The stage decides how long treatment takes.

## Step 2: The visit

- An exam of any sores or rashes
- Blood tests: a treponemal test and an RPR or VDRL titer
- An HIV test, and tests for other STDs
- A pregnancy test if pregnancy is possible
- Treatment chosen by the clinician for your stage. For later stages, treatment can involve more than one visit

## Step 3: The first day

Some people get **fever, chills, headache or aches** within 24 hours of starting treatment. This reaction is common and usually passes within a day. **If you're pregnant**, call your provider if you have contractions or notice the baby moving less.

## Step 4: Partners

Partners need to be tested and are often treated even before results. How far back to go depends on the stage, from about 3 months to a year or more. Your clinician or health department can help, including anonymous notification. See [telling a partner](/guides/telling-a-partner/).

## Step 5: Follow-up blood tests

Repeat RPR or VDRL tests check that the titer falls. Read [follow-up blood tests after syphilis](/syphilis/follow-up-rpr/).

## Avoid while healing

- Sex, until sores and rashes are fully healed and partners are treated
- Touching or picking at sores
- Missing follow-up tests or later-stage visits
