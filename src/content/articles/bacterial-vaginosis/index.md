## BV, trich or yeast?

| | BV | Trichomoniasis | Yeast infection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discharge | Thin, white or gray | Frothy, yellow-green | Thick, white, like cottage cheese |
| Smell | Fishy, often stronger after sex | Often fishy | Usually none |
| Itching | Mild or none | Common | Strong |
| Is it an STD? | No | Yes | No |

Symptoms overlap, and you can have more than one at once. A clinic test or lab-processed swab is the only reliable way to know.

## Causes and risk factors

BV happens when "good" lactobacillus bacteria drop and other bacteria overgrow. Risks include:

- A new sex partner or multiple partners
- Douching
- Not using condoms
- A female partner with BV

## Why treatment matters

BV can raise the risk of:

- Getting chlamydia, gonorrhea, trichomoniasis and HIV
- Pelvic inflammatory disease after gynecological procedures
- Preterm birth, if you're pregnant

## Getting treated

BV is treated with prescription medicine from a clinician. Finish the full course even if symptoms clear, and avoid douching.

Over-the-counter yeast treatments don't treat BV. If you're not sure which you have, get checked.

## When BV keeps coming back

Up to half of people get BV again within a year. If it keeps returning, see a clinician. Recent research has looked at new approaches, including care for partners, and your clinician can tell you what fits your situation.

## Prevention

- Don't douche.
- Use condoms.
- Limit scented soaps and products around the vagina.
