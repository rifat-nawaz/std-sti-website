## Everyday contact: no risk

- Toilet seats
- Towels and bedding
- Kissing, including French kissing
- Hugging family members
- Swimming pools and hot tubs
- Sharing drinks or food

## Non-penetrative contact: some risk

- Genital-to-genital rubbing
- Shared sex toys that aren't washed or covered
- Fingers moving infected fluid to the genitals or eyes (low risk)

## Can virgins get chlamydia?

Someone who has never had penetrative sex can still get chlamydia through other sexual contact, or have had it since birth in rare cases. A clinician can help work out what happened.

## In children

Chlamydia in a child after infancy is a strong sign of sexual contact and must be assessed by a clinician and reported to child protection services. See [chlamydia in children](/chlamydia/children/).
