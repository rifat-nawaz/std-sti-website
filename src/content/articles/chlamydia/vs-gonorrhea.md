## Side by side

| | Chlamydia | Gonorrhea |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | *Chlamydia trachomatis* bacteria | *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* bacteria |
| Reported US cases, 2023 | About 1.65 million | About 600,000 |
| Symptoms | Usually none | Often none in women; more often noticeable in men |
| Discharge | Clear or cloudy, often mild | Often thicker, yellow or green |
| Test | NAAT on urine or swab | Same NAAT, usually the same sample |
| Curable? | Yes | Yes, but resistance is growing |
| Can spread to blood or joints | Rarely | Yes (disseminated infection) |

## Can you have both?

Yes. Many people diagnosed with gonorrhea also have chlamydia. That's why they're tested together and why clinicians often treat for both.

## Which is worse?

Both can cause serious complications if untreated. Gonorrhea's growing resistance makes follow-up testing more important. See [gonorrhea](/gonorrhea/) and [chlamydia](/chlamydia/).

## What to do

Test for both after a new partner or symptoms. Throat and rectal swabs are needed after oral or anal sex.

You can also order a [home test kit](/go/k1/), collect your sample yourself and mail it to a certified lab.
