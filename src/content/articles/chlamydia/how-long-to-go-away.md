## Typical timeline

| When | What usually happens |
|---|---|
| First few days of treatment | Burning and discharge start to ease |
| End of treatment | Most symptoms are gone |
| 7 days after finishing | Safe to have sex again if partners are treated and symptoms are gone |
| About 3 weeks after | A test can be accurate again, if you need one |
| About 3 months after | Retest for reinfection |

Pelvic or testicle pain can take longer to settle than burning or discharge.

## How you know it's gone

For most people, finishing treatment as prescribed is enough. A **test of cure** isn't routinely needed, except:

- During pregnancy
- If symptoms don't go away
- If you might have missed treatment or been reinfected

## Why you shouldn't retest too soon

NAAT tests detect bacterial genetic material. For about **3 weeks after treatment**, they can still find material from dead bacteria. A positive result in that window doesn't necessarily mean treatment failed.

## When to go back

- Symptoms haven't improved a week after finishing treatment
- New symptoms appear
- You had sex with an untreated partner
