## How chlamydia works in the body

*Chlamydia trachomatis* is a bacterium that can only grow inside human cells. It attaches to the cells lining moist areas of the body, gets inside, multiplies, and then bursts out to infect nearby cells.

That process causes inflammation. Sometimes you feel it as burning or discharge. Often you don't feel anything.

## Which body parts it infects

| Body site | How it gets there |
|---|---|
| Cervix | Vaginal sex |
| Urethra (penis or vulva) | Vaginal, anal or oral sex |
| Rectum | Receptive anal sex, or spread from the vagina |
| Throat | Oral sex (less common) |
| Eyes | Touching the eye with infected fluid, or at birth |

## Bacteria or virus?

Chlamydia is caused by **bacteria**. That matters because bacterial infections can be cured, unlike viral STDs such as herpes or HIV, which are managed long term.

## STD or STI?

Both terms are used. "STI" (sexually transmitted infection) is often preferred by clinicians because many infections cause no disease or symptoms. On this site we use both.

## Why it matters

Even without symptoms, chlamydia can spread to the uterus and fallopian tubes and cause lasting damage. Read the full [chlamydia guide](/chlamydia/) for symptoms, testing and prevention.
