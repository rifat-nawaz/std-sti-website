## Key numbers (CDC, 2023)

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Reported cases | 1,648,568 |
| Rate per 100,000 people | 492.2 |
| Share of cases aged 15 to 24 | 55.8% |
| Change from 2022 | Less than 1% |

You can also order a [home test kit](/go/k1/), collect your sample yourself and mail it to a certified lab.

## Who is most affected

- **Young people:** more than half of cases are in people aged 15 to 24, which is why the USPSTF recommends yearly screening for sexually active women 24 and under.
- **Women vs men:** women have higher reported rates. Much of this gap reflects more screening of women; men are more often missed.
- **Gay and bisexual men:** rectal and throat infections are common and often found only through site-specific testing.

## What the numbers miss

Surveillance counts **diagnosed and reported** cases. Many infections are never diagnosed because they cause no symptoms. Changes in testing, such as during the COVID-19 pandemic, also affect how many cases are found.

## State-by-state data

The CDC publishes rates by state and county in its annual STI surveillance report. Rates vary widely between states.

## What it means for you

If you're sexually active and under 25, or have a new partner, test for chlamydia at least once a year. See [who should get screened](/chlamydia/screening/).
