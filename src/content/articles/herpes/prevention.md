## What works

| Method | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Condoms and dental dams | Lower the chance of skin contact with the virus |
| No sex during outbreaks | Shedding is highest when sores or tingling are present |
| Daily medicine for the partner with herpes | Reduces outbreaks and shedding; ask a clinician |
| No oral sex with a cold sore | Prevents HSV-1 spreading to the genitals |
| Talking openly | Lets both partners make informed choices |

## What doesn't work

- **Washing or douching after sex**
- **PrEP for HIV**: it doesn't prevent herpes
- **Supplements or "immune boosters"**: none are proven to prevent herpes
- **Checking for sores alone**: herpes can spread without visible symptoms

## Talking with a partner

Tell partners before sex, in a calm moment. Explain that herpes is common, manageable and often mild, and what you both can do to lower risk. See [telling a partner](/guides/telling-a-partner/).

## Pregnancy

If you're pregnant and your partner has herpes but you don't, tell your prenatal provider. Getting herpes for the first time late in pregnancy carries the highest risk to the baby, so avoid sex that could expose you during the third trimester unless your provider advises otherwise.
