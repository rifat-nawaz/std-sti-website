## Do

- **Keep the area clean.** Wash gently with plain water once or twice a day, then pat dry with a clean towel.
- **Keep it dry.** Moisture slows healing. Loose cotton underwear and loose clothing help.
- **Use a cool compress** wrapped in a cloth for a few minutes at a time. Never put ice directly on skin.
- **Make peeing easier.** Pee in a warm bath or shower, or pour lukewarm water over the area while you pee. Drink plenty of water.
- **Rest.** Fatigue and stress can make outbreaks worse.
- **Wash your hands** after touching the area.

## Avoid

- **All sex**, including oral sex, from the first tingle until sores have fully healed
- **Touching or picking** at sores, then touching your eyes
- **Tight clothing** and synthetic underwear
- **Scented soaps, bubble baths and sprays**
- **Creams or home remedies** not suggested by a clinician or pharmacist
- **Sharing towels** during an outbreak

## When to get help

- It's your first outbreak: a swab within 48 hours confirms the diagnosis
- Outbreaks are frequent or severe
- You're pregnant
