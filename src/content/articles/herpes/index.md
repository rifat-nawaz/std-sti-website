## What is genital herpes?

Genital herpes is an infection with the **herpes simplex virus (HSV)**. There are two types:

- **HSV-1:** usually causes cold sores on the mouth, but can spread to the genitals through oral sex.
- **HSV-2:** usually causes genital herpes and tends to cause more frequent outbreaks there.

After infection, the virus stays in the body for life and can reactivate from time to time.

## Symptoms

Most people have mild symptoms or none at all. When symptoms happen:

- **First outbreak:** small blisters that break into painful sores, usually 2 to 12 days after exposure. Fever, body aches and swollen glands are common. Healing can take 2 to 4 weeks.
- **Later outbreaks:** usually milder and shorter. Many people feel tingling or itching a day or two before sores appear.

## Testing

| Test | When to use it | How reliable |
|---|---|---|
| PCR swab of a sore | You have a sore now | Most accurate; also shows HSV-1 or HSV-2 |
| IgG blood test | No sore, but a specific reason to check, such as a partner with herpes | Can take up to 16 weeks after exposure; low positives often false |
| IgM blood test | Not recommended | Unreliable |

The CDC doesn't recommend herpes blood tests for people without symptoms. For HSV-2 blood tests, low positive index values (1.1 to 3.0) should be confirmed with a second test.

## Getting treated

There's no cure for herpes, but it can be managed. A clinician can prescribe medicine to shorten outbreaks or to prevent them, and will explain which approach fits you.

- **Don't use leftover or someone else's medicine.**
- **See a clinician during a first outbreak,** ideally within 48 hours of sores appearing, so a swab can confirm the diagnosis.
- **Get care quickly** if sores are severe, you can't pee or you have a headache and stiff neck.

## Herpes and pregnancy

The biggest risk to a newborn is when the parent **first gets herpes late in pregnancy**. Tell your prenatal provider if you or your partner have herpes. They will plan how to lower the risk to your baby around delivery.

## Lowering the chance of spreading it

- Avoid sex during outbreaks and when you feel early tingling.
- Ask a clinician whether daily medicine is right for you.
- Use condoms every time.
- Avoid oral sex when you have a cold sore.
