## Who to tell

| Infection | Tell partners from the last... |
|---|---|
| Chlamydia, gonorrhea, trichomoniasis | 60 days, or your most recent partner if longer ago |
| Syphilis | 3 months plus the length of symptoms for early syphilis; longer for later stages |
| HIV | Ask your clinician or health department; it can be years |
| Herpes | Current and future partners, so they can make informed choices |

## How to say it: step by step

1. **Get the facts first.** Know the name of the infection, that it's treatable, and where to test.
2. **Pick a private, calm moment**, or use a message if that's safer.
3. **Say it plainly.** Don't apologize for having an infection.
4. **Give next steps:** where to test, and that treatment is quick for most STDs.
5. **Give them time.** They may need a day to respond.

## Sample messages

**In person or by phone:**
> "I need to tell you something about my health. I tested positive for chlamydia. It's common and easy to cure, but you should get tested and treated too, even if you feel fine."

**By text:**
> "Hey, I just found out I have gonorrhea. You might have it too, even without symptoms. It's curable. A clinic or health department can test you this week."

**For a past partner:**
> "We haven't talked in a while, but I tested positive for an STD and wanted you to know so you can get checked. Let me know if you want info on where to test."

## Anonymous options

- **Health department partner services:** staff contact your partners and tell them they may have been exposed, without saying who you are. Ask the clinic that diagnosed you, or call your local health department.
- **Expedited partner therapy (EPT):** in 49 states, your clinician can give you medicine or a prescription for your partner, for chlamydia and gonorrhea, so they can be treated without a visit.

## After you tell them

- Don't have sex until you've both finished treatment and waited the recommended time, usually 7 days.
- Get retested about 3 months after treatment.
