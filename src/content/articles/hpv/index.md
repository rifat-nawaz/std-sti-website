## What is HPV?

HPV is a group of more than 200 related viruses. About 40 types spread through skin-to-skin sexual contact, including vaginal, anal and oral sex.

| Type group | Examples | What they can cause |
|---|---|---|
| Low-risk | HPV 6 and 11 | Genital warts |
| High-risk | HPV 16 and 18, and others | Cervical, anal, penile, vaginal, vulvar and throat cancers |

## Symptoms

Most HPV infections cause **no symptoms**. When they do:

- **Genital warts:** soft, flesh-colored bumps, flat or raised, single or in clusters. They aren't usually painful.
- **Cancers:** develop over years or decades. Early stages usually have no symptoms, which is why screening matters.

## Testing and screening

**People with a cervix**, following USPSTF guidance:

| Age | Screening |
|---|---|
| 21 to 29 | Pap test every 3 years |
| 30 to 65 | HPV test every 5 years, a Pap and HPV test together every 5 years, or a Pap test every 3 years |
| Over 65 | Usually stop if past results were normal |

Some HPV tests now let you collect the sample yourself with a swab, with your provider's guidance.

**People with a penis:** there's no FDA-approved HPV test. Warts are diagnosed by looking at them. Some clinicians offer anal Pap tests for people at higher risk of anal cancer, such as people with HIV.

## Treatment

There's no treatment for the virus itself. Treatment targets what it causes:

- **Warts:** prescription creams you apply at home, or freezing, removal or other treatments at a clinic. Warts can also go away without treatment.
- **Abnormal cervical cells:** monitored or removed, depending on how advanced they are.
- **Cancers:** treated by specialists.

## The HPV vaccine

The vaccine protects against the HPV types that cause most HPV cancers and genital warts.

- **Recommended at 11 or 12**, and can start at 9.
- **Everyone through age 26** who wasn't fully vaccinated.
- **Ages 27 to 45:** talk with a clinician.
- **Two doses** if started before 15, **three doses** if started at 15 or older.

Vaccinated people with a cervix still need screening, because the vaccine doesn't cover every high-risk type.

## Lowering your risk

- Get vaccinated.
- Use condoms and dental dams. They lower, but don't remove, the risk, because HPV can infect skin they don't cover.
- Keep up with cervical screening.
- Don't smoke. Smoking makes it harder for the body to clear HPV.
