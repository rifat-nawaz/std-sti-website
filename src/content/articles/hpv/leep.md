## Before the procedure

- It's usually scheduled when you're not on your period.
- Tell your clinician if you could be pregnant.
- Arrange to take it easy for the rest of the day.

## During

1. A speculum is placed, as for a Pap test.
2. The cervix is numbed.
3. A thin wire loop removes the abnormal area. The tissue goes to a lab.
4. The area is sealed to reduce bleeding.

## Recovery

| Expect | For about |
|---|---|
| Mild cramping | 1 to 2 days |
| Watery, pink or brown discharge | 2 to 3 weeks |
| Light spotting | Up to a few weeks |

## Avoid until your clinician says it's healed

- Sex
- Tampons and menstrual cups
- Douching
- Baths, swimming and hot tubs
- Heavy lifting or intense exercise for the first few days

## Follow-up

Your clinician will review the lab results and arrange follow-up HPV or Pap tests, usually starting about 6 months later.

## Future pregnancy

Most people have healthy pregnancies after LEEP. There's a small increase in the risk of preterm birth, so tell your prenatal provider you've had one.
