## What is trichomoniasis?

Trichomoniasis is an infection with the parasite *Trichomonas vaginalis*. It spreads through vaginal sex and genital contact. It most often infects the vagina, vulva and urethra.

## Symptoms

About 70% of people have no symptoms. When symptoms appear, they usually start 5 to 28 days after infection, but can begin much later.

| Women | Men |
|---|---|
| Thin, frothy discharge that may be clear, white, yellow or green | Usually none |
| Fishy smell | Itching or irritation inside the penis |
| Itching, burning, redness or soreness | Burning after peeing or ejaculating |
| Discomfort when peeing or during sex | Some discharge |

These symptoms can look like [bacterial vaginosis](/bacterial-vaginosis/) or a [yeast infection](/yeast-infection/). Only a test can tell them apart.

## Why treatment matters

Untreated trich can:

- Raise the risk of getting or passing on HIV
- In pregnancy, raise the risk of preterm birth and a low-birth-weight baby

## Testing

The best test is a **NAAT** on a vaginal swab or urine. Older microscope tests miss many infections.

**Ask for trich specifically.** Many standard panels test only for chlamydia, gonorrhea, HIV and syphilis.

The CDC recommends yearly testing for women with HIV, and testing for anyone with symptoms or a partner with trich.

## Getting treated

Trichomoniasis is **curable**. A clinician prescribes the treatment, which can differ for men, women and during pregnancy.

After a diagnosis:

1. **Don't self-treat.** Leftover or online antibiotics can be the wrong medicine, and a failed treatment lets the infection keep spreading.
2. **Follow your clinician's instructions** and finish everything they prescribe, even if you feel better.
3. **Avoid sex** until you and your partners have finished treatment and your clinician says it's safe.
4. **Tell recent partners** so they can be tested and treated. See [how to tell a partner](/guides/telling-a-partner/).
5. **Women should be retested within 3 months.** About 1 in 5 people get trich again within that time.

## Prevention

- Use condoms every time.
- Make sure partners are tested and treated.
- Ask for a trich test when you get an STD panel.
